Componentwise convexification can impose a local convergence cost on nonconvex quadratically constrained quadratic programs (QCQPs). We derive a lower bound on the relative tangent excess forced by negatively curved components with positive Lagrangian weights. QCQP examples attain this bound. We characterize corrections confined to normal directions. Established sensitivity formulas convert this excess κ into the factor κ/(1 + κ), reproduced by controlled trajectories. LazyQCQP reuses difference-of-convex corrections, refines components and preserves signed quadratic-equality curvature. A specified filter algorithm has stationary accumulation points under explicit assumptions. At a regular Karush--Kuhn--Tucker (KKT) limit, a sufficiently small curvature threshold makes additional diagonal regularization unnecessary. On 4,078 QCQPs, six configurations are compared with Ipopt using an independent original-KKT certificate. With MUMPS for both solvers, the baseline LazyQCQP configuration (R3) certifies 98.9% of 1,037 cases with quadratic inequalities and no quadratic equality, versus 97.1% for monotone Ipopt with certificate-matched stopping targets. On 797 mixed cases with both quadratic constraint types, the rates are 82.8% versus 53.1%. MA27 reverses the first ordering (96.0% versus 97.2%) but preserves the second (83.2% versus 55.0%). For instances certified by both solvers, with both process times above one second, the median ratio of R3 process time to Ipopt process time with MUMPS is 0.34 in the first class and 0.48 in the mixed class. A controlled radio-beamforming ablation shows that removing quadratically implied bounds lowers attained objectives but can reduce certificate counts. On 12 instances, reuse preserves trajectories and reduces median paired process times by approximately 15% with either linear solver.
